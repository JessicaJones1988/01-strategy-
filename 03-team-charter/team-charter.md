## Name the situation
- **Who they are (role, not name), what you have observed, and how long it has been happening.:** I have a team member who is responsible for managing several product development initiatives and coordinating work across multiple departments and vendors. Over the past several months, I have observed that some projects move forward without consistent follow-up, and action items sometimes remain open until another team member or I follow up on them. There have also been instances where deadlines or next steps were not clearly communicated to the individuals involved, resulting in additional follow-up from me to keep projects moving. The team member is engaged in meetings and completes assigned tasks, but the level of proactive communication, ownership of next steps, and prioritization has varied depending on the project. This has been occurring for approximately six months and has become more noticeable as the team's workload and number of active initiatives have increased.

## Make a diagnosis
- **Your diagnosis, plus one sentence on why. Is your frustration with their behavior, or with a decision you made?:** Feedback: My frustration is primarily with the repeated behavior, but I also recognize that my decision to continue stepping in and completing follow-up myself may have allowed the pattern to continue rather than addressing it directly.

## Write your opening line
- **One next action I will take in the next two weeks is:** Schedule a one-on-one conversation with the team member to discuss the recurring pattern of missed follow-up, unclear next steps, and the need for me to step in to keep projects moving.
- **The first sentence of the conversation I need to have is:** “In several recent product initiatives, I’ve noticed that follow-up on action items and communication of next steps has not consistently happened without my involvement, which has required me to step in and has made it harder for the team to keep projects moving efficiently.”

## AI role-play
- **After you step out: what did the role-play change about how you will open this conversation for real?:** I would eventually give them a concrete example. Not to build a case against them, but to eliminate ambiguity. I would address my own part. By repeatedly stepping in, I may have unintentionally taught them that if something doesn't move, I'll move it. Hold them accountable without making the conversation one-sided.

The overall message becomes:

“I trust you to own this work. I will support you, but I won't routinely take the ownership back from you.”

## Refine and complete your charter
- **What We Own. What this team owns.:** Product strategy, roadmap priorities, and outcomes for the Foundations initiative, with a primary focus on field adoption and the mobile experience. We own prioritization against the agreed OKRs and ensure the product delivers a fast, simple field workflow that can replace the phone camera + group text.
- **What is out of scope.:** Enterprise features, customer-specific customizations, and adjacent product areas that do not directly support the Foundations strategy or current OKRs. Renewal requests do not automatically become roadmap priorities.
- **Cross-boundary decisions that need a joint call.:** Any decision that requires shared Engineering capacity, changes an approved quarterly Rock, affects both field adoption and enterprise outcomes, or involves a significant customer renewal or contractual commitment.
- **How We Decide. Who decides feature and scope calls.:** PMs decide day-to-day feature and scope decisions within their approved roadmap. The Foundations Product Leader decides prioritization and trade-offs across the initiative. Engineering decides technical implementation and feasibility; Product decides what is prioritized.
- **How cross-team conflicts escalate.:** PMs first resolve the conflict directly within 1 business day using customer impact, strategic alignment, effort, and OKR impact. If unresolved, escalate to the Foundations Product Leader within the next business day with the trade-off documented. No Engineering capacity is redirected until the decision is made.
- **Who resolves escalations from outside the team.:** The Foundations Product Leader resolves external escalations involving roadmap priority or competing Product/Engineering demands. Significant contractual or material renewal issues are escalated by the Product Leader to the appropriate executive sponsor with the trade-offs documented.

## Show and swap your team charter
- **Where does the charter leave room for interpretation that could cause a conflict?:** Consider adding a tie breaker for joint approvals. Material changes to quarterly commitments require executive sponsor approval.
